LIMITATIONS AND INTERPRETATION
------------------------------
1. The dataset is strongly imbalanced, with Slight accidents forming
   the majority class.

2. The selected model has the highest macro F1 among the four models
   tested, but its advantage over class-weighted Logistic Regression
   is modest.

3. Class-weighted Logistic Regression has higher Fatal recall
   (0.6146) and Serious recall (0.2968) than the selected Random Forest
   (0.5877 and 0.2485 respectively).

4. Fatal precision for the selected model is low. Many records
   predicted as Fatal are not Fatal in the recorded labels.

5. The model predicts recorded collision severity from the available
   features. These results do not establish causality or guarantee
   accurate real-world prediction.

6. Evaluation uses a chronological 2025 test set. Changes in reporting,
   coding, data quality, and road conditions may affect generalisation.

7. The selected model is the best by macro F1 among the four models
   tested on this split; it is not proof that Random Forest is
   universally superior. Further tuning and validation may change
   the ranking.

FINAL DECISION
--------------
Use the class-weighted Random Forest as the main model to align with
the base-paper approach. Retain class-weighted Logistic Regression
as a benchmark and report both models' class-wise results.

The hotspot screening and spatial analysis are separate components.
High-count grid cells should not be described as statistically
validated hotspots unless a formal statistical hotspot test is applied.
