PROJECT METHODOLOGY AND REPRODUCIBILITY NOTES

Project title
-------------
Spatiotemporal Hotspot Mining and Severity Prediction of Road
Accidents using Apache Sedona and Spark MLlib

Generated: 2026-10-23 22:23:29

1. STUDY AREA AND STUDY PERIOD
------------------------------
Study area: Great Britain
Study period: 2021–2025
Spatial reference system for grid analysis: EPSG:27700
Baseline grid size: 5 km × 5 km

The Great Britain boundary was sourced from the Office for National
Statistics Local Authority District boundaries (May 2026) and stored
in the project as a GeoJSON file. The boundary was validated and
transformed to EPSG:27700 for projected spatial analysis.

2. DATA SOURCE AND PREPARATION
------------------------------
The collision dataset is the UK Department for Transport STATS19
road-safety collision dataset covering 2021–2025. The original
dataset contains 513,801 records.

Records with valid coordinate pairs were processed using Apache
Sedona spatial functionality. A spatial filter against the Great
Britain boundary retained 513,252 collision records for the main
spatial analysis. Records outside the boundary and records with
missing coordinates were handled separately.

The spatial join associated collision points with historic local
authority boundaries. The project recorded 513,250 matched records
and 498 unmatched records in the authority join. These counts refer
to different processing stages and should not be added together
without accounting for the missing-coordinate and boundary filters.

3. SPATIOTEMPORAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------
Annual, monthly, hourly, and severity distributions were examined.

For grid-based analysis, collision coordinates were projected to
EPSG:27700 and aggregated into 5 km × 5 km grid cells. The baseline
screening rule used the 95th percentile of occupied grid-year
accident counts, corresponding to a count threshold of 64.

The baseline analysis contained 7,615 occupied grid cells and 1,566
high-count grid-year records. Temporal categories were assigned by
comparing screening results across 2021–2025.

Category totals:
- Never high-count: 7,229 cells
- Persistent: 257 cells
- Variable: 68 cells
- Emerging: 34 cells
- Declining: 27 cells

Sensitivity analyses compared grid sizes of 2 km, 5 km, and 10 km
and count thresholds at the 85th, 90th, and 95th percentiles.

A high-count cell is a screening result. It is not, by itself, proof
of statistical significance or elevated crash risk. Traffic volume
or another exposure measure would be required for risk estimation.

4. SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------
The target classes were encoded as:
- 0 = Fatal
- 1 = Serious
- 2 = Slight

A temporal split was used:
- Training period: 2021–2024
- Test period: 2025

The test set contained 101,525 records and was not used to fit
preprocessing, model parameters, or class weights.

Multinomial Logistic Regression was evaluated as a baseline and
with class weighting to address severity-class imbalance.

Baseline metrics:
- Accuracy: 0.7376
- Weighted F1: 0.6270
- Macro F1: 0.2841

Class-weighted metrics:
- Accuracy: 0.4670
- Weighted F1: 0.5365
- Macro F1: 0.3277

Class weighting improved macro F1 and recall for the Fatal and
Serious classes, but reduced overall accuracy and Slight recall.
Fatal precision remained low. The model is an experimental
classification study, not a validated operational safety tool.

The predictors may include circumstances recorded at or after a
collision. Therefore, this setup must not be described as validated
pre-crash prediction.

5. ASSOCIATION RULE MINING
--------------------------
Categorical collision attributes were converted into transactions
and analysed with Spark FP-Growth.

Minimum support: 0.01
Minimum confidence: 0.30
Severity-filtered rules: 3,205

No qualifying Fatal-consequent rules were found under the selected
support threshold. This does not establish that Fatal collisions
have no associated patterns; rare-class frequency and parameter
choices constrain which rules can be returned.

Association rules describe co-occurrence in the records and do not
establish causality or prove predictive value.

6. PERFORMANCE BENCHMARK
------------------------
Spatial grid aggregation was compared between Spark and a local
Python/pandas implementation using 513,748 spatial records.

Grid size: 5,000 metres
Spark configuration: local[2]
Spark shuffle partitions: 8
Measured repetitions: 3

Median runtime:
- Spark: 0.2391 seconds
- Local Python/pandas: 0.0296 seconds
- Observed runtime ratio: approximately 8.07×

These are results for the measured environment and dataset only.
The comparison used in-memory inputs, a single dataset size, and
slightly different timed processing steps. It does not establish
that one approach will be faster at larger scales.

9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- Collision counts do not measure risk without exposure data.
- The grid and threshold choices influence hotspot classifications.
- High-count screening does not demonstrate statistical significance.
- No road-segment network analysis was performed.
- Changes in reporting or coding across years may affect comparisons.
- The severity model has imbalanced class performance and low Fatal
  precision.
- The classification experiment is not causal and is not validated
  for operational deployment.
